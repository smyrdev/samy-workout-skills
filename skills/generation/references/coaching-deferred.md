## Asymmetry correction

- **Large asymmetry** — one side both bigger and stronger — → cut the developed side to
  maintenance (≈ one third of optimal volume) while the weak side trains at full volume.
- **Mixed asymmetry needs differential intensity, not differential volume.** A side that is bigger
  but weaker trains at 85–100% 1RM; smaller but stronger trains at 30–60%. Growth and strength are
  dissociated by intensity.

## Work capacity and autoregulation

- **Fatigue index = (W_first − W_last) / W_first × 100**, where W = load × reps. At constant load
  it reduces to the percentage rep drop-off across sets.
- **Interpretation:** a rep drop under 20% means high work capacity — sets may be added. A drop of
  50% or more with 3+ minutes rest means low capacity — do not add sets; consider extra rest days
  or a deload.
- **Reps across sets should only ever go down.** Rising reps at the same load means the client is
  not training hard enough — an effort problem, not a programming one.
- **Work capacity is independent of the rep/intensity relation.** An 8RM says nothing about
  drop-off; it has to be measured separately.
- **Combine signals before adjusting.** Good-but-not-great progress with high work capacity → add
  a set. Consistently better progress after rest days with low work capacity → reduce volume; they
  are overreaching.
- **Modifiers.** Trained beats untrained, but *stronger* trained lifters have worse work capacity
  than weaker ones. Women have higher work capacity than men below ~80% intensity and lower above
  ~90%.
- **Subjective "feeling the muscle" is not evidence.** Pump, burn and stretch correlate poorly
  with growth. Never select or justify an exercise on mind-muscle connection alone.

## Volume progression

- Change volume by at most 1 set per muscle per week per mesocycle; interindividual variance in
  tolerance is large.
- Energy deficit → cut volume by 20–33% relative to a maintenance or surplus block, unless an
  advanced client on a long cut is holding the same volume — raise it rather than force it.
- Estimating a 1RM from a set (Epley, `1RM = weight × (1 + reps / 30)`), and prescribing %1RM
  rather than reps for clients whose rep/intensity relation is unusual.
